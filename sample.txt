Information Retrieval is the process of finding useful information from a large collection of data. Search engines are one of the most common examples of information retrieval systems. An IR system usually receives a query and searches a collection of documents to identify relevant information. The system then ranks the available results so that useful documents appear before less relevant documents.

Classical information retrieval methods represent documents using terms and numerical weights. Term Frequency measures how often a term appears in a document. Inverse Document Frequency reduces the importance of terms that appear in many documents. TF-IDF combines these two ideas and gives higher weight to terms that are important in a particular document. This approach is simple, interpretable, and useful for many text-processing tasks.

Document summarization attempts to reduce a long document into a shorter version while retaining important information. Extractive summarization selects existing sentences instead of generating new sentences. One simple approach is to represent each sentence using TF-IDF and calculate an importance score from the weighted terms. Sentences with higher scores are considered more relevant to the document.

After the sentences are ranked, the highest-scoring sentences can be selected to form the summary. Restoring the selected sentences to their original positions can make the final result easier to read. Although this method does not understand language as deeply as modern neural models, it provides a clear demonstration of information retrieval, term weighting, ranking, and document processing.

A student-level summarization system can therefore combine document reading, preprocessing, TF-IDF calculation, sentence ranking, and result generation into one practical application. Such a system can work offline and does not require external artificial intelligence services. It also provides a useful foundation for future improvements such as TextRank, semantic similarity, multilingual processing, OCR, and ROUGE-based evaluation.
